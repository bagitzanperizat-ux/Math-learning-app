## 3. Problem Statement

Children aged 4–9 develop mathematical skills at different rates. At an early age, they begin learning to recognise numbers, count objects, understand quantities, and develop the ability to form written numerals.

Some children may benefit from additional visual and interactive practice when learning these foundational skills. Traditional exercises may not always provide an engaging experience, immediate feedback, or opportunities to practise number formation directly on a screen.

This project proposes a story-based mobile application that supports children's mathematical learning through interactive exercises, visual activities, touch-based number tracing, feedback, and game-based progression. The proposed features will be refined through stakeholder discussions and a review of relevant scientific literature.

## 4. Target Users

The primary target users are children aged 4–9, divided into three proposed age groups:

- **Ages 4–5:** number recognition, counting objects, matching quantities to numerals, and tracing numbers on the screen.
- **Ages 6–7:** number composition and introductory addition and subtraction.
- **Ages 8–9:** more advanced arithmetic activities and age-appropriate mathematical problems.

Each age group is planned to have a separate story-based learning journey with age-appropriate activities, progressive difficulty, and a recurring guide character. The character's name has not yet been finalised.

The initial prototype will focus on children aged 4–5. Parents and teachers are secondary stakeholders who may provide feedback on children's learning needs and the usefulness of the application.

## 5. Project Aim

The aim of this project is to design and prototype a story-based mobile application that supports the development of mathematical skills in children aged 4–9 through interactive exercises and game-based learning.

The initial prototype will focus on children aged 4–5 and their foundational number skills.

## 6. Project Objectives

1. Identify children's mathematical learning needs through relevant literature and stakeholder discussions.
2. Review scientific research on early mathematics learning and interactive educational applications.
3. Compare existing mathematics-learning applications and identify their relevant features and limitations.
4. Define the functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed application.
5. Compare suitable technologies and justify the selected technical solutions.
6. Design the application architecture and user interface.
7. Develop an initial prototype focused on children aged 4–5.
8. Prepare a verification and evaluation plan to assess the prototype's functionality and usability.

## 7. Expected Outcomes

The expected project outcomes are:

- A literature review supported by relevant scientific publications.
- Evidence of relevance based on stakeholder discussions.
- A comparison of existing mathematics-learning applications.
- A documented requirements specification.
- A justified technology selection.
- System architecture diagrams.
- An initial user-interface prototype.
- A demonstrable prototype for children aged 4–5.
- A verification and evaluation plan.

## 8. Project Scope

The overall project concept covers a story-based mobile application for children aged 4–9, organised into three age-specific learning journeys. Each journey is planned to include age-appropriate mathematical activities, progressive difficulty, and a recurring guide character whose name has not yet been finalised.

Due to time and resource constraints, the initial prototype will focus on children aged 4–5. The planned activities include:

- Recognising numbers.
- Counting small groups of objects.
- Matching quantities to numerals.
- Tracing numerals on the screen using touch input, following a visual guide.
- Receiving feedback after completing activities.
- Progressing through simple levels and receiving rewards, where included in the final prototype.

The number-tracing activity is intended to give children an opportunity to practise the basic strokes used to form numerals. The initial design will focus on tracing along a guide rather than automatically recognising and evaluating freely handwritten digits.

The project includes requirements analysis, literature review, comparison of existing applications and technologies, architecture design, interface prototyping, and verification planning.

The complete implementation of all three age groups, automatic recognition of freely handwritten numerals, and a production-ready educational platform are outside the scope of the initial prototype. These features may be considered for future development.
