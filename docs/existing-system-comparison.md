# Existing System Comparison

## 1. Introduction

The purpose of this comparison is to examine existing educational mathematics applications and identify their main features, strengths, and potential limitations in relation to the proposed project.

The comparison focuses on five applications: Khan Academy Kids, Todo Math, DragonBox Numbers, Moose Math, and SplashLearn. These applications were selected because they provide different approaches to mathematics education, including structured practice, game-based learning, number sense development, and curriculum-oriented activities.

Eight criteria are used to compare the applications:

1. Target age group.
2. Mathematical content.
3. Game-based learning.
4. Learning progression and difficulty.
5. Feedback and guidance.
6. Progress monitoring.
7. Accessibility and availability.
8. Relevance to the proposed project.

The comparison is based on publicly available product descriptions and official information. It evaluates advertised features rather than independently measured learning effectiveness.

## 2. Overview of Existing Applications

### 2.1. Khan Academy Kids

Official website: https://www.khanacademy.org/kids/math

Khan Academy Kids is a free educational application for children aged approximately 2–8. It combines mathematics with literacy, language, creative activities, and social-emotional learning.

Its mathematics content includes counting, number sense, addition, subtraction, shapes, measurement, patterns, and problem-solving. The application uses interactive activities, visual models, guidance from characters, and a personalized learning path. Teachers and parents can also access progress information.

**Strengths:** Broad educational coverage, free access without advertisements for families, guided activities, and personalized learning.

**Potential limitation for this project:** The application covers several subjects, so its overall experience is broader than a mathematics-only prototype. Its extensive curriculum may also make it difficult to use as a direct model for a small, narrowly focused first prototype.

### 2.2. Todo Math

Official website: https://www.todomath.com/

Todo Math is designed for children in preschool and the early elementary grades. It offers interactive mathematics activities covering counting, number ordering, arithmetic, shapes, measurement, and other foundational topics.

The application uses game-based activities, rewards, structured learning levels, and options for selecting learning levels. Its public product information also describes accessibility features, including a left-handed mode and support for multiple languages.

**Strengths:** Broad foundational mathematics coverage, structured levels, game elements, and accessibility-oriented features.

**Potential limitation for this project:** Its range of activities and curriculum is substantially broader than the initial prototype. The proposed project will initially focus on a smaller group of skills for children aged 4–5.

### 2.3. DragonBox Numbers

Official website: https://dragonbox.com/products/numbers

DragonBox Numbers is designed for children aged approximately 4–8 and focuses on developing number sense. Its characters, called Nooms, represent numbers and act as interactive mathematical objects.

The application uses visual and playful interactions to help children explore numbers and their relationships. Rather than focusing only on memorising number facts, it aims to help children understand how numbers work.

**Strengths:** Strong focus on number sense, distinctive visual representations, and an integrated game-based approach.

**Potential limitation for this project:** Its main focus is number understanding. The proposed application intends to combine number recognition, counting groups of objects, matching quantities to numerals, and guided numeral tracing in one initial learning sequence.

### 2.4. Moose Math

Official website: https://www.duckduckmoose.com/educational-iphone-itouch-apps-for-kids/moose-math/

Moose Math is an early-learning mathematics application for children approximately aged 3–7. It presents mathematical activities through playful scenarios involving counting, addition, subtraction, sorting, geometry, and other foundational concepts.

Children complete activities and earn rewards that can be used to build and decorate a virtual city. The application also provides a report-card feature for parents and teachers to monitor progress.

**Strengths:** Familiar contexts, multiple game activities, rewards, and progress information for adults.

**Potential limitation for this project:** Its activities cover several mathematical domains. The proposed prototype will concentrate on a clearly defined sequence of foundational number skills and investigate guided numeral tracing as an additional activity.

### 2.5. SplashLearn

Official website: https://www.splashlearn.com/

SplashLearn offers mathematics and English learning activities for children from preschool through Grade 5. Its mathematics content includes interactive games, practice activities, and curriculum-oriented learning experiences.

The platform combines educational content with game-based presentation and offers a wide range of activities for different learning levels.

**Strengths:** Broad age coverage, extensive content, game-based activities, and curriculum-oriented learning.

**Potential limitation for this project:** Its large scope differs from the proposed prototype's narrower focus. A smaller application may be easier to organise around a limited set of skills for a specific age group.

## 4. Critical Comparison

The comparison shows that existing applications use several approaches to mathematics education.

Khan Academy Kids offers broad learning coverage and combines mathematics with other developmental areas. Its guided activities and personalized learning path provide useful design references. However, the proposed application will initially concentrate on mathematics rather than a broad collection of subjects.

Todo Math demonstrates how structured levels, interactive practice, and rewards can be combined. Its approach is relevant to the proposed use of progressive difficulty. Nevertheless, the initial prototype will implement only a limited set of foundational activities.

DragonBox Numbers places particular emphasis on number sense and the relationship between numbers and visual representations. This is relevant to the proposed activities that connect numerical symbols with quantities. Its approach also highlights the importance of making mathematical concepts understandable rather than relying exclusively on memorisation.

Moose Math provides examples of mathematical practice within familiar scenarios. Its rewards and report-card functionality illustrate ways of making activities engaging and communicating progress. The proposed application may draw on these ideas while maintaining a simpler initial scope.

SplashLearn demonstrates how mathematical content can be organised across multiple grades. Its breadth is useful as a reference for possible future expansion, although the initial prototype will target younger children and a smaller number of skills.

A common feature across these applications is the use of interactive activities to support mathematics learning. However, the applications differ in their educational scope, presentation, progression, and focus.

The comparison does not establish that any of these applications lacks a particular feature. In particular, the public descriptions reviewed for this document do not provide sufficient evidence to determine whether guided numeral tracing is entirely absent from every competing application. This feature should be investigated through further product testing if it is to be presented as a differentiator.

## 5. Identified Design Opportunity

Based on the comparison, the proposed project will investigate a focused learning experience that combines several foundational mathematical activities within one application.

The initial prototype will include:

1. Number recognition.
2. Counting small groups of familiar objects.
3. Matching quantities to numerals.
4. Guided numeral tracing.

The activities will be designed for children aged 4–5, with simple visual instructions and a gradual progression in difficulty. Familiar objects and game elements may be used to support engagement.

The intended contribution is not to claim that the proposed application is universally better than existing products. Instead, the project will develop and evaluate a focused prototype based on a clearly defined set of learning objectives.

Future development may extend the content for children aged 6–9. Such expansion will require additional activities appropriate to the mathematical skills and developmental needs of older children.

## 6. Limitations of the Comparison

This comparison is based primarily on official product descriptions rather than a controlled hands-on evaluation. Consequently, it cannot establish which application produces the best learning outcomes or is easiest for children to use.

Some features may differ between platforms, subscription levels, regions, and application versions. Public product descriptions also do not always provide enough detail to compare feedback mechanisms or learning progression precisely.

A future evaluation could involve testing selected applications against a predefined set of tasks and recording observations about usability, instructions, feedback, difficulty, and the visibility of progress.

## 7. Conclusion

The comparison demonstrates that several established applications already support early mathematics through games, structured practice, visual representations, and feedback.

Khan Academy Kids provides a reference for guided learning and broad early mathematics content. Todo Math illustrates structured practice and game elements. DragonBox Numbers focuses on number sense, Moose Math combines familiar scenarios with rewards, and SplashLearn provides a broader curriculum-oriented approach.

These applications provide useful design references but do not eliminate the need to develop and evaluate the proposed prototype. The project will focus on integrating number recognition, counting, quantity matching, and guided numeral tracing into a coherent learning experience for children aged 4–5.

The next stage will be to define functional and non-functional requirements, specify acceptance criteria, and ensure that the prototype's features can be evaluated systematically.
